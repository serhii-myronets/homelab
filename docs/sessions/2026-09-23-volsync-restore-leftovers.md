# Stop VolSync's restore volumes from leaking

The session began as a question about replacing OpenEBS, VolSync and the
snapshot controller: the owner disliked the constant controller errors and
suspected the three cost too much. Measuring both suspicions moved the work
somewhere else.

The three components use 151 MiB of RAM and 11 millicores between them - 1.7%
of the node, against kube-apiserver's 1477 MiB and four applications at roughly
700-800 MiB each. `top node` reporting 80% counts page cache; `MemAvailable`
was 4.2 GiB of 11.4. Replacing the storage stack entirely would free less than
Actual occupies, so it was not worth doing for resources.

The errors split in two. Most were `the object has been modified` from
`pvc_protection_controller`, `pv_protection_controller` and VolSync itself -
ordinary optimistic-concurrency retries that succeed, logged at error level,
generated by eight ReplicationSources creating and deleting temporary claims
every hour. Nothing fixes those and nothing needs to.

The rest were real and had been running unattended since morning: four Released
PVs, one per service built on 2026-09-22, each pinned by the VolumeSnapshot
its own restore had produced, with csi-provisioner retrying the deletion every
two minutes and failure counters above 200. Deleting the four snapshots cleared
the PVs within three minutes and freed 11 GiB of thin pool. All eight services
carried a `latestImage`; the first four had been cleaned by hand on 2026-09-20,
which is why only these four were left.

`cleanupTempPVC` was then dropped from `components/backed-up-volume` so the
ninth service does not repeat it. The reasoning, the measured cost and the
rejected alternatives - a janitor CronJob, deleting the ReplicationDestination
after its restore, and switching to Kopiur - are in `decisions/0024`.

Two things were checked and turned out not to be true. The leftover snapshot is
not orphaned: VolSync sets a controller reference to the ReplicationDestination,
which Flux keeps alive for ever. And an empty R2 repository changes nothing
about the leak - the mover prints `No eligible snapshots found`, exits
successfully, and the snapshot of the empty volume is taken regardless.

Verifying the backup documentation afterwards turned up two things it had got
wrong. `backups.yaml` and `beelink.yaml` both still described four covered
services on one minute, four months of services out of date; both are corrected
and the list now lives in `backups.yaml` alone. And `actual`, added on
2026-09-22, had been given library's minute - two movers on :35, against the
component's own instruction and the throttling it was written to prevent.
`actual` moved to :45, so the eight now run five minutes apart.

The session ended somewhere else again: the owner asked to put the storage
stack in one namespace. OpenEBS turned out not to be movable - its driver
reads OPENEBS_NAMESPACE from its own pod and keeps twenty LVMVolume records
beside itself - so only the two stateless controllers moved, into `storage`.
That needed the Helm ownership annotations on eight kept CRDs repointed by
hand before Flux installed, or the install is refused after the old release is
already gone; both the move and that trap are recorded in `decisions/0025`.

MinIO was considered and does not apply: it is a backup target, not an
orchestrator, and its community edition entered maintenance mode in December
2025. Garage remains the live option should a second, local backup target ever
be wanted alongside R2.
