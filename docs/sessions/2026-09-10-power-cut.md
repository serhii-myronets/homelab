title: A power cut, and the two unrelated failures it left behind
tags: [tailscale, router, proxmox, core, outage, dns]

---

Services were gone. One cause, two failures with nothing in common, and the
diagnosis only came apart once each host was tested separately.

**Core had not come back.** Its ARP entry was `FAILED` on the router and
`incomplete` on the Mac, and ports 22, 80, 443, 9000 and 8080 were all
silent. Nothing above layer 2 was worth looking at — the host was simply off.
The router, on the same outage, had rebooted itself and was up for 3:23. Core
was powered on by hand; consider `Restore on AC Power Loss` in its BIOS, or
the next outage costs another trip to the box.

**Proxmox was cut off by the router's routing table, not by the outage.** The
host was healthy the whole time: from the router, `ping -I br-lan.10
10.1.1.100` answered in 0.6 ms with no loss, while an unforced ping to the
same address lost every packet. `ip route get 10.1.1.100` returned
`dev tailscale0 table 52`, and Tailscale showed Proxmox as
`tx 378612 rx 0` — talking into the tunnel and hearing nothing back.

This was the trap diagnosed on 2026-09-08, returning. What is new is why it
returned, and it makes the recorded fix worthless:
`/usr/bin/gl_tailscale:331` runs `tailscale up --reset --accept-routes` every
time the service starts. `--reset` discards the stored preference and
`--accept-routes` puts `RouteAll` back to true, so
`tailscale set --accept-routes=false` cannot survive a reboot and never
could. Editing that script is out — [decisions/0007](../decisions/0007-router-config-is-not-ours-to-edit.md).

That leaves the approval as the only lever that holds, so the owner
un-approved Proxmox's `10.1.1.0/24` in the Tailscale admin console. Table 52
dropped to peer `/32` routes, `ip route get 10.1.1.100` moved to
`dev br-lan.10 src 10.1.1.1`, and Proxmox came back online in the tailnet.
`RouteAll` is still true on Flint and that is now harmless: there is nothing
approved for it to accept. Proxmox still advertises the route, unapproved.
The price is `tailscale_to_lab`, which is now `no`; WireGuard still reaches
the lab.

All fourteen `.home` hostnames answer — thirteen with 200, Jellyfin with its
usual 302.

**Not reproduced.** The first request to `vm-agent.home` after core booted
failed with `unable to get local issuer certificate` and the retry returned
200; its certificate had been minted seconds earlier. The obvious reading is
a cold-start race in on-demand issuance, and it does not survive testing — a
never-before-seen `*.home` name returned 404 three times running with no TLS
error at all, so on-demand issuance is not the mechanism. This is the second
sighting of the certificate warning left open on 2026-09-07 and the second
time it would not reproduce. Still open.
